### 1. Verify the Rhode Island electric leaf-blower rebate before a planned equipment purchase

**Classification:** Conditional capital-efficiency lead; verify before spending. Owner: Nate. Priority: first only if a blower purchase or replacement was already plausible. Geography: RI commercial use; Smithfield is not one of the five municipalities listed for the additional asthma-related rebate.

**Verified facts.** OER's main page, updated June 30, 2026, advertises the lower of $1,500 or 75% for eligible equipment. It states eligible purchases begin April 27, 2026, and repeatedly posts a June 16, 2027 application close. One paragraph still says June 16, 2026. The displayed $147,401.03 balance is explicitly dated June 30, not today. [S1]

The FAQ says applications follow purchase, awards are not guaranteed, and previous awards count toward a five-rebate lifetime cap. It lists business-operation evidence and up to two additional batteries per blower. Its purchase-start date still says August 25, 2025. [S2]

**Inference for Nate.** A rebate could lower the net cost of a needed tool. No savings, runtime, workload fit, eligibility, or available balance is established for WeedEater. A subsidy alone is not a reason to buy.

**Next action and deliverable.** Prepare a one-page purchase checkpoint: existing blower inventory; replacement need; prior rebate count; proof-of-business type; one dealer quote; full cash price; battery capacity/runtime requirements; written OER answers resolving purchase-date and deadline conflicts and today's funding status. Initial research timebox: 45 minutes. If contacting OER is authorized, ask: “For a new WeedEater purchase, which purchase-start and application dates apply, what funds remain, and which equipment/battery/retailer documentation is required?” Do not transmit tax or ID documents during an initial inquiry.

**Experiment.** If a dealer offers a no-cost demonstration, compare one realistic cleanup segment using the candidate tool against existing equipment. Record elapsed crew time, recharges/swaps, finish quality, and operator assessment. Use an actual quote, not a modeled retail price. The main page specifies new equipment bought through a retailer with a Rhode Island physical store, upfront payment, and a three-year ownership/use commitment. [S1]

**Go criterion.** All eligibility/date/funding questions resolved; actual full cash price fits Nate's existing equipment budget even if reimbursement fails; demonstration finishes the representative segment to current quality without materially reducing crew productivity. Use the rebate only as contingent upside.

**Stop rule.** Park after the 45-minute document review if there is no existing equipment need; no purchase on an unresolved date/funding question or failed demonstration. No automatic purchase when an answer arrives.

**Economics and unknowns.** Verified actual new spending: none. Quote, tax treatment, charger eligibility, battery runtime, funding balance, reimbursement outcome, existing rebates, and qualification are unknown. Proposed checking time: 45 minutes; demonstration time only if useful and available. `verified_deadline = null`; posted June 16, 2027 remains an unconfirmed date because the official pages conflict. No urgency countdown.

### 2. Validate a one-workflow AI implementation service through Rhode Island's business network

**Classification:** Low-capital validation hypothesis beyond landscaping; no verified buyer, partnership, or open procurement. Owner: Nate. Limit: one pilot at a time; protect WeedEater's paid service capacity.

**Verified local signal.** The RI AI Hub Business page, updated September 7, 2026, says it is engaging business owners and industry groups and describes a summer partnership with RISBC for responsible-AI training. [S3] Its contact page, updated September 17, explicitly invites people interested in helping deliver or receive training and offers routing to relevant partners. [S4] RISBC's published cohort RFP includes practical workflow building, coaching, and outcome reporting; its June 12, 2026 proposal deadline is expired. It is evidence of a historical local program, not a live contract. [S5]

**Inference for Nate.** The network is a plausible place to learn whether service businesses need hands-on implementation after training. Nothing proves that it will refer customers, pay Nate, accept him as a trainer, or prefer his offer to existing providers. Nate's own verified workflow evidence must precede claims of expertise.

**Concrete offer to test.** “Turn customer inquiry messages into an owner-reviewed job-intake sheet and draft reply for one cleaning or other local service business.” Extract the address, requested work, timing, missing questions, and next action; preserve source references and flag missing details. Use the client's approved scope/pricing rules. Owner approves every price, schedule commitment, and outgoing message. Deliver a working manual-assisted process, a short operating guide, and before/after timing on real authorized examples. This does not require a new multi-tenant product.

**Next action and deliverable.** Prepare a two-page proof packet using synthetic or properly authorized deidentified records: ten sample inquiries, completed intake sheets/draft replies, failure examples, a measured baseline plan, scope limits, and one-page pilot terms. Include only Jarvis/WeedEater outcomes actually verified by the operator. Proposed preparation cap: two hours, no new paid tools. Prepare—but do not send—a short inquiry through the AI Hub's published contact path asking whether implementation support remains a need and whether there is an appropriate introduction or future program channel. The page invites such contact; it does not award permission to use participant lists.

**Demand experiment.** After outreach is authorized, seek five discovery conversations within 14 days. Ask for a recent inquiry that took too long, the existing process, measured weekly time spent, and who can buy. Continue only if two owners independently show the same recurring problem and at least one agrees to a paid pilot. Proposed price hypothesis: $650 for one workflow and up to ten inquiry examples over seven days; it is neither a quoted market rate nor an ROI claim. Adjust scope before offering if the work cannot fit the economics below.

**Go criteria.** One actual paid pilot; at least 90% correct required-field extraction on the client's ten examples; zero unapproved external actions; measured median owner review time at most two minutes; and the buyer agrees the result solves their documented problem. All sales, setup, support, and correction time must be counted. Require `(collected pilot revenue − direct pilot expenses) / total owner hours >= $130` before repeating commercially; this is a proposed opportunity-cost screen, not the verified definition of WeedEater's target.

**Stop rule.** Stop before building integrations if fewer than two of five owners show the same problem, no one buys by the end of the 14-day test, or preparation exceeds two hours without a usable proof packet. Pause immediately after any material false fact, unapproved message, or privacy/access failure. Stop after one pilot if correction/support time breaks the economic gate. Free workshop attendance or praise does not count as demand.

**Economics and unknowns.** Actual new capital spent: none. Proposed pre-demand spend: $0. Any API budget would need a defined cap before implementation. Delivery effort, access to prospects, client data permission, integration feasibility, willingness to pay, provider costs, and support burden remain unknown. OpenAI's documentation supports schema-constrained output but expressly says such output can still contain mistakes; capability is not customer demand. [S6]
